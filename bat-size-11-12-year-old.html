What Size Bat for an 11- or 12-Year-Old? 30–31 Inches, Drop -10

Most 11- and 12-year-olds swing a 30- to 31-inch bat at drop -10, about 20–21 ounces. Eleven and twelve share the same size range, which is why they share this page: the difference between the two ages is the drop, not the length. An 11-year-old is almost always at drop -10; a strong 12-year-old is ready to step to drop -8. Use the height and weight chart below rather than the age alone. The short answer: 30 inches, drop -10, for most 11-year-olds; 31 inches, drop -10 (or -8 for a strong hitter), for most 12-year-olds. USSSA stamp for 11U/12U travel, USA Baseball stamp for Little League. Between two sizes, go shorter — a bat they can control beats a longer one they drag through the zone.

What drop?

Drop is length minus weight: a 30-inch bat at drop -10 weighs 20 ounces; at drop -8 it weighs 22 ounces. Start an 11-year-old at drop -10 and a 12-year-old at drop -10, stepping to -8 if they are strong for their age and the -10 feels light — if they can hold the bat out to the side for 10 seconds without the barrel dipping and still get the barrel through the zone on time, they are ready for -8. Don't buy drop -5 at 11 or 12: that's a 13-year-old's bat, and a too-heavy bat teaches a long, late swing that's hard to unlearn. A smaller 11-year-old — under about 75 pounds — is the exception: 29 inches at drop -10 (or -11 if the swing drags) is the right start there.

Which stamp do they need?

Little League and most rec leagues: USA Baseball stamp. USSSA (1.15 BPF) if your 11- or 12-year-old is playing 11U/12U travel ball — but a USSSA bat is still illegal in a Little League game, so if your player does both, buy USA. Confirm the stamp on the barrel before you buy, and confirm it with your coach or league.

Two fit tests before you buy

The hold test: have your player hold the bat straight out to the side with one hand for 10 seconds. If the barrel end dips, it's too heavy. The hip test: stand the bat on the ground next to your player — the knob should reach about hip height, not the chest. If it reaches the chest, it's too long.

Mistakes to avoid at 11 and 12

Don't buy 32–33 inches to "grow into it" — a too-long bat is the single most common sizing mistake at this age, and it teaches a bad swing. Don't buy drop -5 yet — that's a 13-year-old's bat, and most 12-year-olds can't swing it on time. Don't buy a USSSA bat if they also play Little League — it's illegal there, so a player who does both needs the USA stamp. Don't buy a $350 composite as a first travel bat — the CATX2 alloy above does the same job for a season or two at half the price. And don't size up just because a teammate did — the chart and the hold test beat copying a bigger kid's bat.
