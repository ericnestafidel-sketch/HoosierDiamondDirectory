What Size Bat for a 9-Year-Old? 28–29 Inches, Drop -10 to -11

Most 9-year-olds swing a 28- to 29-inch bat — the single most common recommendation is a 28-inch bat at drop -11, about 17 ounces. Nine is the bridge year: the last season before 10U kid pitch, and the age where drop starts to matter. A strong 9-year-old can handle drop -10; a smaller one should stay at -11 or -12. Charts put the range at 27–29 inches, so use the height and weight chart below rather than the age alone. Full bat sizing guide · Bat rules, plain English

The short answer: 28 inches, drop -11, USA Baseball stamp (USSSA if 9U or 10U travel). Between two sizes, go shorter — a bat they can control beats a longer one they drag through the zone.

What drop?

Drop is length minus weight: a 28-inch bat at drop -11 weighs 17 ounces; at drop -10 it weighs 18 ounces. Start a 9-year-old at drop -11 and step to -10 only if the bat feels too light and the swing stays quick. If the bat feels heavy and the swing drags, stay at -11 or go one step lighter (-12). Don't buy drop -8 at 9 — that's a 12-year-old's bat, and a too-heavy bat teaches a long, late swing that's hard to unlearn.

Which stamp do they need?

Little League and most rec leagues: USA Baseball stamp. USSSA (1.15 BPF) if your 9-year-old is playing 9U or 10U travel ball — but a USSSA bat is still illegal in a Little League game, so if your player does both, buy USA. Confirm the stamp on the barrel before you buy, and confirm it with your coach or league.

Two fit tests before you buy

The hold test: have your player hold the bat straight out to the side with one hand for 10 seconds. If the barrel end dips, it's too heavy. The hip test: stand the bat on the ground next to your player — the knob should reach about hip height, not the chest. If it reaches the chest, it's too long.

Mistakes to avoid at 9

Don't buy 30–31 inches to "grow into it" — a too-long bat is the single most common sizing mistake at this age, and it teaches a bad swing. Don't buy drop -8 — that's too heavy for most 9-year-olds. Don't buy a $350 composite — a 9-year-old outgrows it in one season and can't tell a composite from an alloy. Don't buy a USSSA bat if they also play Little League — it's illegal there. And don't buy a tee-ball bat for kid pitch — tee-ball bats are 26 inches or shorter by rule.
