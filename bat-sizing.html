Youth Bat Sizing Guide: Length, Drop & Stamp by Age

Three numbers decide the bat — stamp, length, drop — in that order. Get the stamp wrong and the other two don't matter: a $300 bat with the wrong stamp is a $300 practice bat.

1 · The stamp decides first

Every non-wood bat carries a certification stamp on the taper, just above the grip. The three that matter in Indiana youth baseball are not interchangeable — a USSSA bat is not legal in a USA Baseball league, and a USA bat gives up pop in USSSA play.

StampWhere it's requiredMax barrelDrops
USA BaseballLittle League, Cal Ripken / Babe Ruth, PONY and most rec leagues2⅔″Any (usually −5 to −13)
USSSA 1.15 BPFTravel ball and USSSA tournaments, 12U and under2¾″Any (−5 to −13); 13U is −8 or heavier
BBCOR .50High school (NFHS), college, and USSSA 14U — the national standard at 14U since Jan 1, 20262⅔″−3 only

USA Baseball bats are built to perform like wood — less pop, more emphasis on the swing. USSSA 1.15 BPF bats are the hottest of the three, with the bigger 2¾″ barrel. BBCOR bats are always −3 and always 2⅔″; a 33-inch BBCOR weighs at least 30 ounces. One more change to know: starting in 2028, high school adds lighter −4, −5 and −6 options alongside −3, so the jump to high school weight gets a ramp. For the 2027 season, high school is still −3 only.

Still deciding between stamps? The Bat Rules page settles USSSA vs. USA Baseball in two minutes.

2 · Size by age

Age is the starting point — height and strength move a player up or down a size from here. Length is measured knob to barrel end; drop is length minus weight in ounces.

Drop, worked out: a 30-inch bat that weighs 20 ounces is a −10. A bigger drop means a lighter bat for its length. Default to −10 for anyone under 12, and go −11 or −12 for a smaller or younger player without a second thought.

3 · Size by height

4 · Two fit tests, at home

  • The hold test. Player holds the bat by the handle with the bottom hand only, arm straight out to the side at shoulder height. If the barrel starts dipping before about 30–45 seconds, it's too heavy.
  • The swing test. Watch a real swing: if the barrel drags behind the hands, the bat is too long or too heavy. Bat speed beats barrel size at every youth age — when in doubt, go lighter.
The expensive mistakeBuy for the league you're in this season, not the one you hope to join next year — and don't spend $400 on a −5 for a 13U player who will need a BBCOR −3 at 14U. Check the stamp on the barrel before you pay, not after.
