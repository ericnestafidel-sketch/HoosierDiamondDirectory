Youth Gloves: Size by Age & Who Should Buy What

Gloves are sized in inches, measured from the tip of the index finger down to the heel of the palm. Catcher's mitts are the exception — they're measured around the outside (circumference). Start with age below, then narrow it by position: an 11″ infield glove and an 11″ outfield glove are not the same glove.

1 · Glove size by age group

The ranges below line up across the Wilson, Rawlings and Academy Sports size charts. Age is a starting point, not a rule — if your player can't squeeze the glove shut or can't control the tip, size down even if the chart says bigger. A glove they can close beats a glove they grow into.

The 10-second fit testGlove on the non-throwing hand: they should be able to (1) close it around a ball with one hand, (2) hold it up without the tip flopping, and (3) get it off without a wrestling match. 2 · Who should buy what — by position

Same hand, four different tools. Under 9U, ignore all of this and buy one soft all-positions glove. From 10U up, the shape starts to earn its keep.

Infield · 2B / SS / 3B

The small, quick glove

10.75″–11.75″ · Shallow pocket · I-web or single-post web

Infield gloves are the smallest on the field on purpose: a shallow pocket lets the fielder get the ball out and into the throwing hand fast enough to beat the runner. A big, deep glove turns every grounder into a fishing trip.

Buy this if: your player lives in the infield, or plays a bit of everything — this is also the best one-glove compromise.

Skip it if: they're a primary outfielder chasing fly balls at 11U+ — they'll want the reach below.

Example: Wilson A500 11″ at Dick's →
Outfield

The long, deep glove

11.5″–12.5″ youth · Deep pocket · H-web or trapeze web

Outfield gloves are longer, with a deeper pocket to secure a fly ball on the run and extra reach on balls over the head. The open web doubles as a sun visor for high flies — that's a feature, not a style choice.

Buy this if: outfield is their main home at 10U+, or they're tall for their age and play the corners of the grass.

Skip it if: they're under 10 or mostly infield — the extra length is dead weight on a grounder and slows the transfer.

Example: Rawlings Select Pro Lite 11.5″ at JustBallGloves →
Pitcher / Utility

The hide-the-grip glove

11.5″–12″ · Medium pocket · Closed or basket web

Pitchers use a mid-size glove with a closed web so hitters can't see the grip change. That same closed-web, mid-size shape is the best second glove for a utility player who pitches, plays first one inning and outfield the next.

Buy this if: your player pitches regularly at 11U+, or you need one glove to cover three spots for a season.

Skip it if: they never pitch and have a settled infield or outfield spot — buy for that spot instead.

Younger utility pick: Mizuno PowerClose 10.5″ at SPC Sports →
First base · Mitt

The scoop — a mitt, not a glove

11.5″–12″ youth · Long & curved · Extra padding, no individual fingers

A first-base mitt is longer and rounder than a fielder's glove, built to scoop throws in the dirt and stretch for the out without the ball popping out. It only works at first — you can't play shortstop with it, and most leagues won't let you try.

Buy this if: first base is their regular spot (most games, most innings) at 10U+. Their normal glove still comes to every game.

Skip it if: they fill in at first once in a while — a regular fielder's glove handles that fine, and the team bag usually has a mitt.

Example: Rawlings Renegade 11.5″ mitt at JustBallGloves →
Catcher · Mitt

The armour — measured differently

30″–32.5″ around (youth) · Heavy padding · Closed two-piece web

A catcher's mitt has no individual fingers, the heaviest padding on the field, and a deep round pocket for receiving pitches and blocking balls in the dirt. It's required behind the plate in most leagues — and useless anywhere else on the diamond.

Buy their own if: they catch every week. Fit and break-in are personal, and a mitt shaped to their hand receives better than the shared team mitt.

Use the team mitt if: they're trying catcher for a few innings — every league stocks a full catcher's set for exactly this.

Example: Wilson A500 32″ mitt at Wilson.com →
Tee ball / First glove

The easy-close starter

9″–10.5″ · Synthetic or soft leather · Pre-shaped, no break-in

For ages 4–8 the only job is learning to catch without fearing the ball. Look for PowerClose / Sure Catch-style notches and soft shells that a small hand can actually squeeze shut — brand and leather grade don't matter yet, because they'll outgrow it in a season or two.

Buy this if: it's a first glove, or a hand-me-down is too big or too stiff to close.

Skip the upgrade: a $90 leather glove buys a 6-year-old nothing a $25 soft glove doesn't — save the money for the 10U glove.

Breaking it in (the honest way)

Play catch. That's the whole secret — 20 minutes a few times a week shapes a pocket better than any oven, microwave, or mallet trick, and it doesn't cook the leather. A ball in the pocket with the glove tied shut overnight between sessions helps. Most youth gloves are game-ready in one to two weeks of real use; “pre-broken-in” models are ready day one but wear out a season sooner.

Right-hand throw or left-hand throw?The listing names the throwing hand. “Right-hand throw” (RHT) means the glove goes on the left hand — that's most players. Left-handed throwers need LHT and have fewer youth options, so order earlier in the season before sizes sell out.
