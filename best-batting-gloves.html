Best Batting Gloves for Youth Baseball: Sizing & 5 Picks

Batting gloves are the cheapest piece of gear a parent gets asked for by name — and the easiest to buy wrong. The size on the tag means nothing across brands: the same hand is a youth large in one brand and a youth medium in another. Measure the hand, buy the glove that matches the chart, and spend $16 on the first pair, not $65. Best fielding glove for 10U → · Training tools →

The short answer: measure from the base of the palm to the tip of the middle finger, then use that brand’s own chart. Franklin Digitek ($16) for a first pair, Franklin CFX Pro (~$40) as the everyday glove, Bruce Bolt ($65) only once the hand has mostly stopped growing.

Do kids actually need batting gloves?

Not every kid does. Gloves help in three real situations: blisters (a new bat and a lot of swings in a week will tear up a young palm), grip in cold or wet weather (early-spring games in Indiana are exactly this), and sting on mishits. If your player takes 20 swings a week and never complains, skip them — the money is better spent on a tee or a bucket of balls. If they are hitting most days, or they have already had a blister, gloves are a $16 fix. One rule to check before game day: some tournaments restrict what color batting gloves a pitcher can wear, and the rule changes by organizer — check your event’s rules if your player pitches.

Size by hand length, not by age

Measure from the base of the palm (where the wrist crease is) to the tip of the middle finger. Then match the number to the brand’s chart — do not guess from age or from another brand’s size. A 6.4-inch hand is a youth large on Franklin’s chart and a youth medium on Easton’s and Bruce Bolt’s.

Gloves run tight on purpose — a batting glove should fit like a second skin, with no loose material bunching in the palm. If it is between sizes, size up: Amazon flags the CFX Pro as frequently returned for tight fit. And buy one pair per player, not one pair to share: two hands in the same glove stretch it out for both of them.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not buy by age or by another brand’s size — measure the hand and use that brand’s chart, or the glove will be the wrong size. Do not buy the $65 glove for a growing hand — a 9-year-old will outgrow a Bruce Bolt by fall, and the $16 Digitek does the same job that season. Do not share one pair between two players — it stretches out for both of them. Do not wear a brand-new pair on game day without trying them on first — Amazon flags the CFX Pro as frequently returned for tight fit, so check the fit at home first. And do not pay full price in November — batting gloves are one of the most-discounted youth items in the Black Friday sales.
