The three kinds of rec baseball

Most Indiana towns have one of these three. They all play the same game — the difference is who writes the rules and where the all-star path leads.

Little League

Ages 4–16 · Tee Ball through Senior League

The biggest name in rec baseball, and the one most parents have heard of. Divisions run Tee Ball (4–7), Minor and Major (roughly 7–12), then Intermediate, Junior and Senior up to age 16. Your league is set by where you live or go to school — there are boundaries, so use the League Finder below to find yours. The all-star path runs district → state → the Great Lakes Region and, for 12U Majors, the Little League Baseball World Series.

Babe Ruth / Cal Ripken

Cal Ripken ages 4–12 · Babe Ruth ages 13–18

One organization, two halves. Cal Ripken is the younger half: T-Ball (4–6), Rookie (7–8), Minor (9–10) and Major (11–12), with the popular Major/70 division on 70-foot bases — an easier step up to the full-size field. Babe Ruth is the older half, ages 13–18, played on the same 90-foot diamonds as high school ball. If your player ages out of Little League at 12 or 13 and is not ready for travel, Babe Ruth is the natural next stop. Indiana sits in Babe Ruth League's Ohio Valley region.

Independent community leagues

All ages · Run locally, own rules

Not every Indiana town has a Little League or a Babe Ruth charter — and plenty of towns do just fine without one. Independent leagues are run by a local parks & recreation department or a volunteer board, with their own rules, their own fields, and usually the lowest fees of the three (often the cheapest option in town). They do not feed into a national all-star tournament, which is exactly why some families prefer them: it is just baseball, close to home, with neighbors. If your town has no affiliated league listed above, your parks & rec department is the first place to ask.

How to pick one

Start with your address

Little League assigns you by where you live or go to school. Check the League Finder first — if it gives you a league, that is your league.

Ask about ages and cost

Ask the league which division fits your player's age, what the fee covers, and what gear you need to buy. Rec fees are usually $75–$200.

Check the schedule

Ask how many nights a week, where games are played, and when the season runs. If it fits your family, it is the right league.

Sign up early

Most rec leagues open registration in January and close in mid-March. Late sign-ups usually mean a waitlist, not a team.

Can't find your town?

Three places to check, in order: (1) the Little League League Finder — type your home or school address and it tells you if a Little League covers you. (2) Babe Ruth League — Indiana is in the Ohio Valley region; the site can point you to your state commissioner, who knows every chartered league. (3) Your town or county parks & recreation department — if there is an independent league, they run it or know who does. And if your league is missing from this page or listed wrong, tell us and we will fix it.
