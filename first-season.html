Your Kid's First Season: A Parent's Guide

Registration is the easy part. Here's the rest of the first season, in the order it actually happens.

1. Rec or travel? Start with rec.

Rec league costs a fraction of travel ball, practices close to home, and every kid plays. It's the right first step for almost everyone — travel teams recruit out of rec leagues, so a strong rec season is also the tryout tape. Travel makes sense once your player asks for more games, not before. To find one near you, start with our Indiana rec leagues list — 117 leagues across 40 counties, searchable by town and county. If your town isn't listed there, try Little League's League Finder or check your parks & rec department.

2. Evaluations are not the SATs

Most rec leagues run a short skills evaluation so teams draft evenly — coaches watch kids hit off a tee, field grounders, and throw. Nobody gets cut from rec. Show up with a glove that fits and let them play; the draft sorts itself out.

3. What the league provides vs. what you buy

Typically provided: jersey and hat, team bats and helmets, catcher's gear. You buy: a glove, cleats, gray or white pants (check the league's color), and a bat bag if you want one. The full list is on the starter kit page.

4. The concession stand is the social network

Nearly every rec league runs on parent volunteers — scorekeeping, field prep, and yes, concession shifts. It's also where you learn everything: which coach teaches fundamentals, when tryouts happen, who's selling last year's cleats. Sign up for a shift early; it's the best information trade in youth sports.

5. What “good” looks like at 8

Not the scoreboard. A good first season ends with your player understanding where the play is, wanting to come back next year, and owning one skill that's visibly better than in April. Everything else is noise.

The one-page versionRegister early (spots and sibling discounts go fast), buy the glove first, and read what the season really costs before anyone talks you into a travel tryout.
