What Size Bat for an 8-Year-Old? 28–29 Inches, Drop -11

Most 8-year-olds swing a 28- to 29-inch bat at drop -11 — a 29-inch bat at drop -11 weighs about 18 ounces, and that's the sweet spot for an average 8-year-old (about 4 feet 2 inches, 56 pounds). Charts disagree by an inch because some run shorter than coach-pitch and kid-pitch charts: a 7-year-old's chart tops out at 28 inches, while an 8-year-old's runs to 29 or 30. If your 8-year-old is in coach pitch or Little League Minors, 28–29 inches is the answer. Full bat sizing guide · Bat rules, plain English

The short answer: 29 inches, drop -11, USA Baseball stamp. Between two sizes, go shorter — a bat they can control beats a longer one they drag through the zone.

What drop?

Drop is length minus weight: a 29-inch bat at drop -11 weighs 18 ounces. Start an 8-year-old at drop -11. If the bat feels heavy and the swing drags, go one step lighter (-12); a strong player who finds -11 too light can step to -10. Don't buy drop -8 at 8 — that's a 12-year-old's bat, and a too-heavy bat teaches a long, late swing that's hard to unlearn.

Which stamp do they need?

Little League and most rec leagues: USA Baseball stamp. USSSA only if your 8-year-old is playing 8U travel ball — 8U is the first age where USSSA bats show up, and a USSSA bat is not legal in a Little League game. Confirm the stamp on the barrel before you buy, and confirm it with your coach or league.

Two fit tests before you buy

The hold test: have your player hold the bat straight out to the side with one hand for 10 seconds. If the barrel end dips, it's too heavy. The hip test: stand the bat on the ground next to your player — the knob should reach about hip height, not the chest. If it reaches the chest, it's too long.

Mistakes to avoid at 8

Don't buy 30–31 inches to "grow into it" — a too-long bat is the single most common sizing mistake at this age, and it teaches a bad swing. Don't buy drop -8 — that's too heavy for most 8-year-olds. Don't buy a $350 composite — an 8-year-old outgrows it in one season and can't tell a composite from an alloy. Don't buy a USSSA bat for Little League — it's illegal there. And don't buy a tee-ball bat for coach or kid pitch — tee-ball bats are 26 inches or shorter by rule.
