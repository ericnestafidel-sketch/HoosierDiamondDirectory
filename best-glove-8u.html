Best Baseball Glove for 8U: 10.5″ to 11″, Picks & Prices

An 8-year-old needs a 10.5 to 11 inch glove — 10.5 inches for most 8-year-olds and infield innings, 11 inches for a bigger 8-year-old or outfield time. This is the first “real” glove for most kids: the soft tee-ball glove is done, but the hand still isn't strong enough for stiff leather or a long break-in. Full glove guide by age & position →

Before you order — the throwing-hand check: a Right Hand Throw (RHT) glove is worn on the left hand. It's still the most common ordering mistake at every age. Confirm which hand your player throws with, then order that throw.

What size glove for an 8-year-old?

Per this site's glove size guide, ages 7–8 (8U, coach and machine pitch): infield 10″–11″, outfield 10.5″–11.5″. First base is an 11.5″ mitt and catchers need a 30″–31″ mitt — both are mitts, not gloves. If your player rotates positions, buy the 10.5″ utility glove and stop there; it works everywhere at 8U.

Does position matter yet at 8U?

No. At 8U every player still rotates, the ball comes off a coach's arm or a machine, and the glove's job is to close easily and catch what's thrown at it. A position-specific glove — a shallow infield pattern, a deep outfield pocket, a closed web for pitchers — doesn't buy anything before 10U. Buy one all-positions glove in the right size. Catchers need a real mitt (about 30″–31″ around at this age) — that's on the full glove guide.

The 10-second fit test

Put the glove on, ball in the pocket, squeeze. If your player can close it on their own in under 10 seconds without using both hands, it fits. If they can't, it's too stiff or too big — size down or pick an easy-close model like the Sure Catch heel design above. A glove that can't be closed is a glove that drops balls. New leather needs a break-in — see how to break in a youth glove — but at 8U, a glove that needs weeks of work before it closes is the wrong glove.
