How Much Does Travel Baseball Cost in Indiana?

One player, one season, 2027 — the all-in number by age, for a local Indiana team and for a regional one. The team fee on the tryout flyer is only the down payment: hotels, gas, gear and lessons are where the real money goes.

Every number below is an estimate range, not a quote. They come from published 2026 cost guides (Mavtrax, Dugout HQ, ATBATS) and the directory's own True Cost page — and they disagree with each other, because programs are priced so differently. Use them to budget and to ask the right questions, then get the team's written fee schedule before you pay a deposit.

All-in per year, gear and travel included. Local = mostly Indiana events, volunteer or semi-paid coaches, few hotel nights. Regional = 8–14 sanctioned events, paid coaching, indoor winter training, several hotel weekends.

The honest totals

Rec season, all-in: roughly $250–$600 once gear is counted. Local travel season: roughly $1,500–$3,500. Regional travel with regular hotels: $4,000 and up — before anyone buys a second bat. And the top end is real: national and showcase programs run $6,000–$15,000+ a year, mostly on flights, hotels and recruiting events. None of that makes travel ball a bad buy; plenty of families get every dollar back in weekends they'll talk about for decades. It just means the decision deserves the real number, which is the entire reason this page exists.

How to keep it cheaper

  • Ask what the fee covers, in writing, before tryouts. Tournaments, uniforms, winter training and umpires are sometimes in the fee and sometimes billed on top — the difference can be $1,000.
  • Start rec, not travel, at 8U. A $75–$200 rec season plus a $30 bat teaches you whether your kid loves the game before you spend travel money finding out.
  • One bat per season, bought on sale. Last year's model of the same bat is the same bat. Check the stamp rules on our USSSA vs USA page before you buy — an illegal bat is the most common wasted purchase.
  • Hand-me-down gear is free gear. Gloves and bats get outgrown before they wear out. A decent youth glove lasts 2–3 seasons and a used one costs half.
  • Coolers and carpools. Gas and food run $100–$250 a weekend; a cooler and a shared ride are the two cheapest line items to cut.
  • Skip private lessons until 11U–12U unless the coach asks for them. Tee work in the yard and a cage membership do more for an 8U or 9U hitter than a weekly lesson.
